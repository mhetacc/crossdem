# Crossdem: Embedding Italian Prime Ministers' Speeches to Track the Evolution of Political Rhetoric Against V-Dem Indices

## Abstract

Politicians are some of the most influential people in history: from the Greeks' demagogues to our Presidents and Prime Ministers, leaders have always tried to shape society. Public discourse is one of the main tools at their disposal, playing a fundamental role in conveying their message, signaling ingroup membership, and gathering voters' support. In this work I collect public speeches from Italian Prime Ministers delivered between 1945 and 2025, scraping them from YouTube and Radio Radicale. The speeches are then transcribed into text with OpenAI's speech recognition model "Whisper", and annotated with the LLM "Gemma 4" for their degrees of hate speech, aggressiveness, and negativity, as well as whether they target a specific group or not. The resulting dataset contains 6,038 speeches distributed among twenty-four Prime Ministers. To explore the relationship between politicians' rhetoric and the quality of democracy, I measure the evolution of lexical diversity over time, comparing it against V-Dem democratic indices. The same comparison is performed on the evolution of speeches' sentiment over time, and I embed the speeches into vectors to visualize and measure polarization among politicians and political leanings. The variability of the lexical diversity declines over the years, with an inversion in the period 2020-2025, but this trend does not appear to be correlated with the evolution of the democratic indices. Likewise, no correlation can be established between speeches' sentiment and democratic indices. The right is more aggressive than both the center and the left, while the left is slightly more negative than both the center and the right, and all political leanings target their political adversaries in seven speeches out of ten. Lastly, the analysis on the speech embeddings suggests the presence of polarization in the Italian political landscape.
